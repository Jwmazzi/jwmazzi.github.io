Wild Potomac

Through Bird City Brunswick, I've been working with Wild Potomac on a grant that will fund tree plantings across the city.

Below are the overview maps I put together for the grant. They were built in QGIS and communicate the planting sites that are in or out of the various zones we were targeting in the grant.

51 of the 60 Google Form responses were selected as potential planting sites.




FOSS4G

This is the second project in the past couple months where I've really spent time figuring out my baseline FOSS4G tools.

Having built a print map for Springfest, I wasn't starting from scratch for this grant with Wild Potomac. QGIS continues to be excellent for quickly generating maps. This time I didn't use Inkscape and stuck with exporting simple layouts from QGIS. I'm still in the process of figuring out how much I want to use PostGIS through Docker in my work. Since this project was relatively simple, working out of a single GeoPackage and doing a mix of manual work in QGIS and writing some Python code was good enough. Figuring out how much of the map production pipeline I can automate in QGIS is likely my next homework.

Google Forms

People love Google Forms. It's free and simple to build something quickly. However, dealing with the free-form text that was used for the planting sites (i.e., home addresses), meant some extra cleanup before we could make maps. For this project, I just wrote a simple script that geocoded (via the Google API) the address field. Using something like Survey 123 would have simplified my life a bit, but the associated cost is prohibitive. Figuring out how Google Forms, or anotherfree/cheap equivalent, could have the spatial piece as a first-class attribute by default would be excellent.
